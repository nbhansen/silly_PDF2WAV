# Plain English Conversion Prompt for HCI Academic Text

## Task
Convert the following academic HCI text into plain English, making it accessible to a general audience while preserving the technical accuracy and core meaning.

## Plain English Principles (from Plain English Campaign A-Z Guide)

### Word Substitution Rules
When you encounter complex academic words, replace them with simpler alternatives:

**Use everyday words that people use in conversation**. If you wouldn't use a word in everyday conversation, look for a simpler alternative.

#### Common Academic-to-Plain Substitutions:
- "utilize" → "use"
- "facilitate" → "help" or "make possible"
- "implement" → "carry out" or "do"
- "demonstrate" → "show" or "prove"
- "establish" → "show", "find out", or "set up"
- "indicate" → "show" or "suggest"
- "obtain" → "get" or "receive"
- "evaluate" → "test" or "check"
- "ascertain" → "find out"
- "substantial" → "large", "great", or "a lot of"
- "commence" → "start" or "begin"
- "terminate" → "stop" or "end"
- "acquire" → "buy" or "get"
- "accomplish" → "do" or "finish"
- "constitutes" → "makes up" or "forms"

#### Avoid These Pompous Phrases:
- "in the majority of instances" → "most" or "mostly"
- "at this moment in time" → "now" (or edit out)
- "with regard to" → "about" or "for"
- "in order that" → "so that"
- "prior to" → "before"
- "subsequent to" → "after"
- "in excess of" → "more than"
- "in accordance with" → "in line with" or "because of"

#### Eliminate Unnecessary Words:
Remove these words that add nothing to the meaning:
- "actually", "basically", "currently", "existing", "extremely", "obviously", "quite", "really", "very"
- "as a matter of fact", "at the end of the day", "in due course", "the fact of the matter is"

### Technical Terms
- Keep essential HCI technical terms but explain them clearly
- When first introducing a technical concept, provide a brief, clear explanation
- Use "you" and "we" to make the text more personal and direct

### Instructions for Conversion:
1. **Replace complex words** with everyday alternatives from the A-Z list above
2. **Eliminate unnecessary phrases** that add no meaning
3. **Keep technical accuracy** - don't oversimplify to the point of losing meaning
4. **Use active voice** where possible
5. **Address the reader directly** using "you" when appropriate
6. **Break down complex concepts** into simpler explanations
7. **Maintain the logical flow** of the original argument

### Sentence Structure Guidelines
**Keep sentences short** - Aim for an average of 15-20 words per sentence:
- Stick to one main idea per sentence, plus perhaps one related point
- Vary sentence length - mix short punchy sentences with longer ones
- Break up long sentences when possible

### Use Active Voice
**Prefer active verbs** - Make your writing crisp and professional:
- Active: "We will consider this matter shortly"
- Passive: "This matter will be considered by us shortly"
- Aim for 80-90% of verbs to be active
- Use passive only when appropriate (to be diplomatic, avoid blame, or when the doer is unknown)

### Personal and Direct Language
**Use 'you' and 'we'**:
- Call the reader "you" even in general documents
- Call your organization "we"
- Write as if talking to someone across a desk
- "You must send us..." instead of "Applicants must send..."
- "We will tell you..." instead of "Customers will be advised..."

### Clear Instructions
**Don't be afraid to give direct commands**:
- Use imperatives (commands) - they're the fastest way to give instructions
- "Send the form" instead of "The form should be sent"
- "Fill in section 3" instead of "Section 3 should be completed"
- Add "please" for politeness, but omit it when something must be done

### Avoid Nominalizations
**Turn abstract nouns back into verbs**:
- "We discussed the matter" instead of "We had a discussion about the matter"
- "Drivers will stop the trains" instead of "There will be a stoppage of trains by drivers"
- "A team implemented the method" instead of "The implementation of the method was done by a team"

### Use Lists Effectively
**Break up information with lists**:
- Use bullet points (not numbers/letters) for clarity
- Two types: continuous sentences with listed points, or separate complete sentences
- For continuous sentences: use semicolons and lowercase letters
- For separate sentences: use capital letters and full stops
- Make sure each point follows logically from the introduction

### Appropriate Language Level
**Choose words suitable for your reader**:
- Use everyday English whenever possible
- Explain technical terms when necessary
- Avoid specialist jargon with general audiences
- Use jargon only when writing to specialists who understand it
